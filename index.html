About

I grew up in Nigeria, where an unstable grid was part of daily life. Voltage swings destroyed appliances, outages shut small businesses, and families planned around when power might return. As a maintenance intern on Airtel base stations I worked next to the diesel generators that kept them running, and started asking whether wind turbines on the towers, backed by good wind forecasts, could replace them. That question became my first research project.

Studying in France, Germany and Spain showed me the other side of the problem. Even advanced grids become fragile when they run close to their stability limits with a high share of renewables. What I took from both places is that reliability comes from engineering: good models, good control, and decisions made with real constraints in view.

Research and teaching go together for me. I tutored physics and electrical engineering through my undergraduate years and taught Arduino programming. As a graduate teaching assistant at Bells University of Technology, I co-supervised bachelor's theses and wrote a laboratory manual on Arduino hardware programming.

Projects

Household electricity demand forecasting with dynamic tariff signals

Master's thesis, Fraunhofer ISE, Freiburg, March to August 2026. Part of the FAME4ME project in the federally funded SmartLivingNEXT programme. Supervisor: Lina Ganter Næss.

Many EU electricity suppliers must now offer dynamic tariffs to customers with smart meters. My thesis asked which households actually respond to those price signals, and whether their demand can be forecast when a household has no metering history at all.

  • Using 15-minute smart meter data from 61 households in a German field trial, I split them into 23 price-responsive and 38 non-responsive households. K‑Means and agglomerative clustering agreed on 58 of the 61 (95%).
  • Checking the segments from three independent angles (tariff response 90%, behaviour 77%, technology flexibility 67%) showed that tariff structure predicts demand response more strongly than technology ownership.
  • A cold-start pipeline replaces missing load history with peer-group aggregate signals plus weather, price and household features. Of seven models benchmarked, the best RMSE was 0.2288 kWh for responsive households (LSTM) and 0.1207 kWh for non-responsive households (XGBoost).
  • Thesis grade 9.5/10. A journal article based on the thesis is in preparation with Fraunhofer ISE.

Smart grid modelling, reliability analysis and multi-solution optimisation

UPC Barcelona, 2025. Team project.

I built the network model, time-series power flow, reliability analysis and cost comparison. A 24-hour AC power flow of a 110 kV network in PandaPower, driven by real Red Eléctrica de España profiles, exposed hourly overloads (Transformer 1 peaked at 119.67%) and undervoltage at 6 buses. Failure-rate models priced downtime and energy not served, and three upgrade options (renewables, BESS‑HVDC, demand response) cut the annual cost from €62.4M to €23.6M.

Electricity market analysis with machine learning

UPC Barcelona, 2025.

A day-ahead Spanish electricity price model reaching R² = 0.882 and RMSE = 14.57 €/MWh. K‑Means and hierarchical clustering separated high, medium and low price regimes and linked them to demand, renewable output and gas costs. PCA cut the feature space from 87 to 43 variables without losing accuracy.

Renewable capacity expansion and scenario optimisation

UPC Barcelona, 2025.

A linear programming model in Pyomo (HiGHS) that co-optimises installed capacity and hourly dispatch of nuclear, gas, PV and wind over 8,760 hours, with real cost data and actual Catalan demand. System cost rose nonlinearly beyond an 80 to 90% renewable share, and a ±50% sensitivity analysis found gas OPEX to be the main driver of system LCOE, followed by wind and PV CAPEX.

Modelling and control of a grid-following voltage source converter

UPC Barcelona, 2025. Individual project.

A 5 MVA, 690 V, 50 Hz converter in MATLAB/Simulink with a PLL, decoupled dq current control and outer power loops, tuned analytically by internal model control. Sweeping the short-circuit ratio from 10 to 1 drove it unstable at SCR 1, with current oscillations reaching ±8,000 A. Impedance-based retuning (Leq = Lc + Lg) and a fivefold cut in outer-loop bandwidth restored stable operation with controlled power derating. This is the case drawn at the top of the page.

Supercapacitor storage for wind power smoothing

UPC Barcelona, 2025.

A 1.5 MW wind turbine with stochastic turbulence in MATLAB/Simulink. A third-order Butterworth filter at 0.4 Hz sets a smooth grid power reference, and a constrained supercapacitor controller with voltage prediction and SOC estimation absorbs the difference. The store is sized at 96 kW / 428 kJ and realised as a 918 V nacelle-mounted pack of 9 modules. Power spectral density analysis confirms the smoothing across the 0.2 to 2 Hz turbulence band.

Electric vehicle modelling and control

HTW Berlin, 2025.

A full electric vehicle model in MATLAB/Simulink with longitudinal dynamics, a PMSM traction motor under field-oriented control, and adaptive cruise control. Wind speed forecasting for Northern Nigeria

LAUTECH, 2023. Final-year thesis.

An LSTM model in TensorFlow that predicts daily wind speed from Nigerian Meteorological Agency data (MAPE 0.0416, R² 0.9987), motivated by the idea of replacing base station diesel generators with hybrid wind, PV and battery supply. It won the Faculty Award for Best Final-Year Thesis and became a journal paper.
